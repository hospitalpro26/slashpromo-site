Stomach & digestive health

When should you consult a gastroenterologist?

Recurring symptoms may need assessment even when an endoscopy is not necessary. A qualified clinician decides which examination or treatment fits your history and symptoms.

Persistent acidity or heartburn

Reflux, burning in the chest or recurring upper abdominal discomfort.

Chronic abdominal pain

Ongoing or unexplained stomach pain, bloating and indigestion.

Difficulty swallowing

Food sticking or painful swallowing requires prompt clinical assessment.

Suspected gastritis or ulcers

Recurring symptoms or concerns about H. pylori and peptic ulcer disease.

Changes in bowel habits

Persistent diarrhoea, constipation or other unexplained intestinal symptoms.

Unexplained anaemia or weight loss

These may warrant investigation based on a clinician's assessment.

What does an upper GI endoscopy examine? A flexible camera examines the oesophagus, stomach and first part of the small intestine. A colonoscopy examines the large bowel. Your clinician determines whether either test is appropriate.
Seek urgent medical care: Vomiting blood, black tarry stools with weakness or fainting, severe sudden abdominal pain, or serious dehydration should be assessed urgently, not through a routine clinic booking.

Frequently asked questions about endoscopy in Sukkur

Do chronic acidity or stomach pain always require endoscopy?

No. Your doctor will consider symptoms, age, medical history and warning signs before recommending tests. An endoscopy is not necessary for everyone with reflux or indigestion.

Is upper GI endoscopy a painful procedure?

Usually not. Before upper GI endoscopy, the throat may be numbed with a local anaesthetic spray or gargle, and an intravenous sedative injection may be given when appropriate to help you relax. Some people still experience brief gagging, pressure or bloating. Sedation is not always used, and the exact approach depends on the patient and facility. Ask your endoscopy team about pain control, sedation and safety before the procedure.

What is the difference between endoscopy and colonoscopy?

Upper GI endoscopy examines the upper digestive tract, including the stomach. Colonoscopy examines the large intestine. They investigate different problems.
