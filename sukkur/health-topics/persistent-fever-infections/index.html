Understanding persistent fever and infections

A fever is often a body temperature of 38°C or higher. Many fevers improve, but a fever that persists, worsens or occurs with other concerning symptoms needs medical evaluation. Fever alone does not identify which infection is present.

Symptoms and questions for your doctor

  • Make a note of temperature readings, how long the fever has lasted and associated symptoms.
  • A clinician may ask about cough, urinary symptoms, abdominal pain, travel, mosquito exposure and sick contacts.
  • Testing for conditions such as malaria, dengue, enteric fever or respiratory infections is based on clinical assessment—not every fever needs every test.
  • Antibiotics are not appropriate for every fever and should not be started or changed without advice.

When to seek urgent medical attention: Seek emergency care for confusion, difficulty breathing, severe dehydration, a stiff neck with severe headache, seizures or collapse. Seek prompt medical advice for fever that is not improving, especially during pregnancy or in people with weakened immunity.

Frequently asked questions

Is every fever a bacterial infection?

No. Viral and non-infectious causes are also possible; assessment determines whether tests or treatment are needed.

When should an adult with fever see a doctor?

If it persists, worsens, or is accompanied by concerning symptoms, arrange assessment promptly.
