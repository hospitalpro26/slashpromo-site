Understanding high blood pressure

High blood pressure (hypertension) often causes no obvious symptoms. Regular blood pressure checks and a clinician’s assessment matter more than waiting for a headache or dizziness. One reading alone may not establish a diagnosis.

Symptoms and questions for your doctor

  • Many people feel well despite raised blood pressure.
  • A doctor may review repeated readings, medicines, diabetes, kidney health and cardiovascular risk.
  • Long-term management may include lifestyle advice and prescribed medicines based on individual circumstances.

When to seek urgent medical attention: If a blood pressure reading is above 180 systolic or 120 diastolic, repeat it after a brief rest and seek urgent medical advice. If accompanied by chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache with neurological symptoms, vision changes, weakness or trouble speaking, go to an emergency department immediately.

Frequently asked questions

Does high blood pressure always cause symptoms?

No. High blood pressure commonly has no symptoms; measuring it is essential.

Should I stop medicines if I feel better?

Do not stop or change prescribed blood pressure medicines without discussing it with your clinician.
