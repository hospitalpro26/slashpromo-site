Kidney stone treatment and urologist consultation

Kidney stone care depends on stone size, location, symptoms, infection and kidney function. A urologist may recommend monitoring and medication for suitable stones, or procedures such as shock-wave lithotripsy or ureteroscopy when clinically indicated. Could this be kidney stone pain?

Kidney stones are hard deposits that form in the kidneys. Pain often begins when a stone moves into the ureter (the tube carrying urine to the bladder) and interferes with urine flow. The pain is sometimes called renal colic and may arrive in waves. Not every backache or urinary symptom is caused by a stone, so proper assessment matters.

Common signs and symptoms of kidney stones

  • Severe side or back pain: often below the ribs, sometimes spreading to the lower abdomen or groin.
  • Pain that comes in waves: the intensity and location can change as a stone moves.
  • Blood in the urine: urine may look pink, red or brown, although blood may not be visible.
  • Pain or burning while urinating: particularly with stones lower in the urinary tract.
  • Frequent or urgent urination: or passing only small amounts of urine.
  • Nausea or vomiting: sometimes accompanying severe pain.
  • No symptoms at all: some small stones are discovered incidentally.

Symptoms overlap with urinary infections and other abdominal conditions; they do not confirm a kidney stone diagnosis.

When kidney stone symptoms are an emergency

Go to the nearest emergency department now if suspected stone pain occurs with:

  • Fever or chills, particularly alongside severe flank pain.
  • Inability to pass urine or a marked fall in urine output.
  • Severe or persistent pain that is not settling, or repeated vomiting preventing fluids.
  • Feeling very unwell, faint, confused or signs of serious infection.

An infected, obstructed kidney can be life-threatening and needs urgent hospital treatment. Do not wait for a routine OPD appointment or rely on this article to make that decision.

What problems can kidney stones cause?

  • Urinary blockage (obstruction): a stone can prevent normal drainage from the kidney.
  • Hydronephrosis: swelling of the kidney from backed-up urine.
  • Urinary tract or kidney infection: serious when combined with obstruction.
  • Reduced kidney function: particularly if obstruction is prolonged, affects both kidneys or occurs in someone with one functioning kidney.
  • Repeated pain or recurrent stones: follow-up can help identify risk factors and reduce recurrence.

Complications are not inevitable. Early assessment helps determine whether observation, medication or a procedure is appropriate.

How are kidney stones diagnosed and treated?

A clinician may check your symptoms, examination, urine tests, kidney function and imaging such as ultrasound or CT when appropriate. The next step depends on the stone's size, position, infection risk, obstruction, pain and kidney function.

  • Small stones: may pass with supervised pain relief, fluids as appropriate and follow-up. Do not force large quantities of water during severe pain or vomiting.
  • Medicines: selected patients with ureteral stones may be offered medication to help passage.
  • Stone procedures: some cases require ureteroscopy, laser fragmentation, shock-wave lithotripsy or percutaneous surgery, depending on clinical assessment and local availability.
  • Emergency drainage: a blocked, infected kidney may need urgent decompression and antibiotics before definitive stone treatment.

There is no single “best” operation for everyone. Discuss benefits, risks and local procedure availability with a urologist.

Frequently asked questions

What does kidney stone pain feel like?

Kidney stones can cause intense, wave-like pain in the side or back that may spread toward the groin. Some stones cause no pain.

Can a kidney stone cause fever?

Fever or chills with suspected kidney stones may indicate infection. Seek emergency medical care, especially if urine flow is blocked.

Does every kidney stone need surgery?

No. Some stones pass without a procedure. Treatment depends on stone size, location, obstruction, infection, kidney function and pain.
