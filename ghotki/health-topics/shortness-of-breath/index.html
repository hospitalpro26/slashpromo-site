Understanding shortness of breath and breathing difficulty

Shortness of breath can come from asthma, COPD, infection, heart problems, anemia and other conditions. New or worsening breathlessness requires careful assessment rather than assuming the lungs are the only cause.

Common signs and symptoms

  • Breathlessness during normal walking or at rest
  • Wheezing, cough, chest discomfort or new reduced activity
  • Breathlessness when lying flat or associated with ankle swelling

What to discuss with a clinician

  • A clinician may check oxygen levels, examine heart and lungs and choose appropriate tests.
  • Note whether symptoms began suddenly, occur with exercise or are linked to chest pain or fever.
  • A chest specialist may assess respiratory causes; urgent assessment may instead be needed for cardiac or other causes.

Emergency warning signs: Sudden or severe breathlessness, inability to speak, heavy chest pain, blue/grey lips, confusion, fainting or coughing blood requires immediate emergency care. For urgent symptoms, visit the nearest emergency department, not a routine OPD or enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

Is shortness of breath always a lung problem?

No. Heart disease, anemia and other illnesses can also cause breathlessness.

What if breathlessness begins suddenly?

Sudden or severe breathlessness needs prompt emergency assessment, particularly with chest pain or fainting.
