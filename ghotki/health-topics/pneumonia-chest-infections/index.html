Understanding pneumonia and chest infections

Chest infections may affect the airways or lung tissue. Pneumonia can cause fever, cough, chest discomfort and breathlessness, particularly in older adults or people with other health conditions.

Common signs and symptoms

  • Cough, fever, chills or feeling unwell
  • Pain on breathing or coughing and new breathlessness
  • Unusual confusion or weakness, especially in older adults

What to discuss with a clinician

  • A clinician may assess oxygen levels and decide if a chest X-ray or other investigations are needed.
  • Viruses and bacteria can both cause respiratory infections; treatment depends on the diagnosis.
  • Avoid unnecessary or self-prescribed antibiotics, and arrange reassessment if symptoms worsen.

Emergency warning signs: Marked difficulty breathing, blue/grey lips, severe chest pain, confusion or collapse requires urgent emergency care. For urgent symptoms, visit the nearest emergency department, not a routine OPD or enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

Does every chest infection need antibiotics?

No. Treatment depends on whether a bacterial infection is suspected and on the person's condition.

When can pneumonia be serious?

Severe breathlessness, reduced alertness, low oxygen or rapidly worsening symptoms require urgent evaluation.
