Understanding persistent cough and chest symptoms

A cough may follow a viral infection, but a cough that persists or keeps returning deserves assessment. Causes can include asthma, upper-airway irritation, reflux, medicines and respiratory infections including tuberculosis.

Common signs and symptoms

  • Cough lasting three weeks or longer
  • Repeated cough with phlegm, wheeze or breathlessness
  • Cough plus weight loss, night sweats, fever or exposure to tuberculosis

What to discuss with a clinician

  • Note the duration, blood in sputum, smoking exposure, fever and any recent contacts with an infectious illness.
  • A doctor may decide whether examination, chest imaging, sputum tests or spirometry are useful.
  • Avoid starting antibiotics or tuberculosis medicines without a diagnosis and clinician guidance.

Emergency warning signs: Coughing significant blood, severe breathing difficulty, fainting or chest pain requires immediate emergency assessment. For urgent symptoms, visit the nearest emergency department, not a routine OPD or enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

When should a prolonged cough be checked?

Arrange an assessment if cough lasts three weeks or more, or sooner if important warning symptoms occur.

Does every prolonged cough mean tuberculosis?

No. A prolonged cough has many possible causes. TB requires appropriate medical testing.
