# Complex Pelvic and Acetabular Fracture Surgery Reported at Sukkur Trauma Centre

*Sukkur, Sindh — Hospital Updates*

An orthopaedic team at the Trauma and Emergency Response Centre Sukkur reportedly carried out surgery for complex pelvic and acetabular fractures, according to a Facebook post published by Abdul Wajid Wadho.

The post describes a patient who sustained serious injuries following a fall from a train. Following assessment and imaging, the surgical team reportedly performed fracture fixation using reconstructive plates and screws. Independent confirmation of the operation's clinical details and the patient's recovery has not yet been obtained.

### Multidisciplinary orthopaedic collaboration

According to the social media account, the surgeons involved included Professor Dr. Zulfiqar Soomro, Dr. Rashed Mahmood Soomro, Dr. Aziz Ullah Bhai, Dr. Saqib Shah and Dr. Raja Lakhiyar. The report also acknowledged Dr. Mahmood Qureshi for administrative coordination.

The post specifically credited Dr. Rashed Mahmood Soomro with travelling from Larkana to support the procedure. These names, roles and contributions should be confirmed with the hospital before the article is published. The source material contains inconsistent spellings of some clinicians' names, so the list is provisional.

### Why these injuries require specialist treatment

The acetabulum forms the socket of the hip joint. Displaced pelvic and acetabular fractures may require complex surgical planning, specialist fixation, and rehabilitation, depending on the patient's injuries and clinical condition. No specific prognosis can be inferred from the images alone.

### Recognition following the reported procedure

The Facebook post also describes an appreciation gathering in which members of the team received Sindhi Ajrak. The event and identities pictured remain subject to verification.

### Source and verification note

This is an attributed report based on a Facebook post by Abdul Wajid Wadho. Slashpromo has not independently confirmed the surgery, the claim that it was the centre's first such operation, or the patient's longer-term outcome.
