Dengue in Sindh: November 2026 Case Reports and Warning Signs

News coverage on 12 November cited Sindh Health Department dengue figures showing substantial transmission in Karachi and elsewhere in Sindh. The original provincial case bulletin was not independently available during this editorial review; figures below are attributed to the report, not presented as independently verified surveillance totals.

Editorial draft — not published

Prepared 22 November 2026 for editorial review. Do not treat the draft as a current live surveillance update or as personalized medical advice.

What was reported

The Express Tribune reported 2,369 dengue cases in Karachi and 2,100 elsewhere in Sindh during 2026 to the reporting date, citing Sindh Health Department figures. Its report also cited 2,055 provincial cases during September and October. These are reporting-period figures, not a live count; check newer official surveillance data before publication or updating. WHO confirms that dengue is transmitted by infected Aedes mosquitoes, and severe illness can occur.

Symptoms and urgent warning signs

Dengue may cause fever, headache, pain behind the eyes, body aches, nausea and rash. Severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding, fast breathing, unusual weakness or cold clammy skin require urgent hospital assessment, including when fever seems to be improving.

Prevention and safer self-care

Reduce standing water around homes, cover water containers, wear protective clothing, use mosquito repellent and keep mosquitoes out of living areas. Anyone with suspected dengue should seek clinical advice, maintain hydration if able to drink, and avoid aspirin and ibuprofen because they can increase bleeding risk. Paracetamol may be used according to medical advice; do not delay evaluation of warning signs.

Frequently asked questions

What are the warning signs of severe dengue?

Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, rapid breathing, or marked weakness require urgent medical care.

Does a falling fever mean dengue is over?

Not necessarily. Severe dengue warning signs may appear after the fever begins to settle.

Are antibiotics routine treatment for dengue?

No. Dengue is a viral infection; antibiotics are not a routine treatment unless a clinician identifies a separate bacterial infection.
