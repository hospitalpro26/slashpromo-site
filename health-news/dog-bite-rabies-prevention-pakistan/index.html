Dog Bite and Rabies Prevention in Pakistan: What to Do Immediately

Dog and other mammal bites can expose people to rabies. WHO guidance emphasizes urgent first aid and prompt post-exposure assessment: do not wait for symptoms or attempt to judge risk from the appearance of a wound alone.

First aid after a bite or scratch

Immediately wash and flush the wound thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes. A suitable antiseptic can be applied afterward if available. Seek urgent medical assessment the same day for possible rabies post-exposure prophylaxis, tetanus prevention and wound care. Do not apply irritants, traditional remedies or delay treatment.

When are rabies vaccine and immunoglobulin needed?

A clinician assesses the type of contact, wound and animal. WHO recommends prompt vaccination after qualifying category II or III exposures; category III includes bites that penetrate skin, significant scratches, saliva contacting broken skin or mucous membranes, and certain bat exposures. Rabies immunoglobulin or recommended monoclonal antibodies may be needed for category III exposure. Previous vaccination affects the regimen and should be disclosed.

Why timing matters

Rabies can be prevented after exposure when effective care begins promptly. Once symptoms develop, rabies is almost always fatal. Children and people with head, face, hand or multiple bite wounds must receive urgent assessment. Do not assume a vaccinated or apparently healthy animal makes medical assessment unnecessary.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I wash a dog-bite wound?

At least 15 minutes with soap and running water, followed by prompt medical care.

Should I wait for the animal to become sick before visiting a hospital?

No. Seek medical assessment immediately after a possible exposure.

Does every dog bite need rabies immunoglobulin?

No. A trained clinician decides based on exposure category and vaccination history; many category III exposures require it.
