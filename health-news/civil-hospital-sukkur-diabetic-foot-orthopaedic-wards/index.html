Civil Hospital Sukkur Inaugurates Diabetic Foot Ward and Renovated Orthopaedic Ward

A new diabetic foot care facility and improvements to an orthopaedic ward were formally unveiled at Civil Hospital Sukkur, according to contemporary news reports.

Event: 18 November 2026   ·   Article published: 21 November 2026   ·   SlashPromo Health Desk

SUKKUR: Member of the National Assembly Syed Khursheed Ahmed Shah inaugurated a newly established Diabetic Foot Care Ward and a renovated Orthopaedic Ward at Civil Hospital Sukkur on Wednesday, 18 November 2026. The development was reported by Associated Press of Pakistan (APP) and other local news coverage.

Expanded facilities for patients

The new diabetic foot care ward is intended to improve access to assessment and treatment for people with foot complications associated with diabetes. The renovation of the orthopaedic ward is aimed at improving the hospital environment for patients requiring care for bone, joint and injury-related conditions.

The inauguration establishes that the facilities were formally opened. It does not independently establish their current staffing, treatment capacity, appointment arrangements or live OPD schedules; patients should confirm these with the hospital.

Hospital administration and invited officials

News coverage identifies Professor Dr. Saleh Muhammad Channa, Principal of Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College (GMMMC), and Dr. Ahmed Mujtaba Memon, Medical Superintendent of Civil Hospital Sukkur, among the hospital leadership associated with the event.

Other attendees reported included senior health and district administration representatives. Coverage describes remarks on strengthening public-sector healthcare and improving services for residents of Sukkur and surrounding areas.

Diabetes awareness alongside the inauguration

The event was also connected with a diabetes awareness programme. Reporting from the hospital described discussions on prevention, timely diagnosis and treatment, as well as the importance of diabetic foot care.

Diabetes-related foot problems can become serious if they are not assessed promptly. People with diabetes who develop a new foot wound, redness, swelling or signs of infection should seek timely assessment from an appropriate healthcare professional.

For patients: This is a report about the inauguration of hospital facilities, not a confirmation of current ward opening hours, appointment availability or specific clinical services. Please verify operational details directly with Civil Hospital Sukkur.
