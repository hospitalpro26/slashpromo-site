Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College Teaching Hospital Sukkur Inaugurates Diabetic Foot and Renovated Orthopaedic Wards

A new Diabetic Foot Care Ward and the renovated Orthopaedic Ward were inaugurated at Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College Teaching Hospital Sukkur, formerly known as Civil Hospital Sukkur, on 18 November 2026.

SUKKUR: Member of the National Assembly Syed Khursheed Ahmed Shah inaugurated the newly established Diabetic Foot Care Ward and the renovated Orthopaedic Ward at Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College Teaching Hospital Sukkur (formerly Civil Hospital Sukkur) on Wednesday, 18 November 2026. The inauguration was reported by Associated Press of Pakistan (APP) and other local news coverage.

Expanded facilities for patients

The new diabetic foot care ward is intended to improve access to assessment and treatment for people with foot complications associated with diabetes. The renovation of the orthopaedic ward is aimed at improving the hospital environment for patients requiring care for bone, joint and injury-related conditions.

The inauguration establishes that the facilities were formally opened. It does not independently establish their current staffing, treatment capacity, appointment arrangements or live OPD schedules; patients should confirm these with the hospital.

Principal, Medical Superintendent and specialist doctors at the ceremony

Professor Dr. Saleh Muhammad Channa is the Principal of Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College (GMMMC), and Dr. Ahmed Mujtabba is the Medical Superintendent (MS) of Ghulam Muhammad Mahar Medical College Teaching Hospital Sukkur. Both attended the ward inauguration.

The following specialist doctors were also present on the occasion:

  • Dr. Lal Chand Dhingra — Consultant Neurophysician
  • Dr. Altaf Ahmed Shaikh — Consultant Physician
  • Dr. Haleem Mirani — Consultant Ophthalmologist

The inauguration highlighted ongoing efforts to strengthen public-sector medical services for Sukkur and surrounding communities.

Diabetes awareness alongside the inauguration

The event was also connected with a diabetes awareness programme. Reporting from the hospital described discussions on prevention, timely diagnosis and treatment, as well as the importance of diabetic foot care.

Diabetes-related foot problems can become serious if they are not assessed promptly. People with diabetes who develop a new foot wound, redness, swelling or signs of infection should seek timely assessment from an appropriate healthcare professional.
