New Diabetes Wearable Tracks Glucose and Ketones Together: FDA 2026 Authorization

The FDA authorized Abbott's Libre Duo 10 Day system to continuously monitor glucose and ketones in people with diabetes aged two years and older.

Published 22 November 2026 · Source-attributed medical technology news

Published 22 November 2026. Reporting is based on the attributed sources below. This article provides general information, not medical advice, and does not imply the technology is available in Pakistan.

What the FDA authorized

On 25 August 2026, the US Food and Drug Administration authorized Abbott Diabetes Care's Libre Duo 10 Day Continuous Dual Glucose Ketone Monitoring System for people with diabetes aged two years and older. It combines two measurements in one wearable device.

Why ketones matter

Ketones can rise when the body cannot use glucose effectively and begins using fat for energy. High ketone levels can be associated with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a potentially life-threatening emergency. Monitoring trends may help patients and caregivers recognize concerning changes earlier, alongside symptoms and clinical advice.

What the wearable measures

According to the FDA, the sensor measures glucose and ketones in fluid beneath the skin and sends readings to a compatible smartphone. It can display trends and provide alerts for concerning ketone levels. This does not replace emergency assessment when a person has vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing, confusion or other signs of DKA.

Regulatory and practical limits

The authorization is a US De Novo decision, not confirmation of availability, price or registration in Pakistan. Readings need clinical interpretation; treatment plans should not be changed solely on the basis of an online news article.

What does Libre Duo monitor?

It continuously measures glucose and ketone levels in interstitial fluid.

No. Trend information may help identify warning signs but does not replace treatment, sick-day plans or emergency care.
