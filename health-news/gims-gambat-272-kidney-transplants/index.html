GIMS Gambat Reports 272 Successful Kidney Transplants

The institute in Gambat, Sindh, says its kidney transplant programme has completed 272 successful transplants and highlights free dialysis, transplant surgery and post-transplant medicines.

GAMBAT, KHAIRPUR, SINDH: Pir Abdul Qadir Shah Jeelani Institute of Medical Sciences (GIMS) has highlighted a milestone in its kidney transplant programme. In a video shared on the institute's official Facebook page, GIMS reported 272 successful kidney transplants.

The post, which acknowledges coverage by Time News TV, also describes a free-of-cost treatment programme covering dialysis, transplant surgery and post-transplant medicines. These service details are based on the institute's public announcement; patients should confirm current eligibility and arrangements directly with GIMS.

Free dialysis and transplant services highlighted

Kidney failure may require regular dialysis or, in suitable patients, a kidney transplant. Both treatment and long-term follow-up can be costly. GIMS's announcement highlights three aspects of its reported programme:

  • Kidney dialysis without treatment charges, as described by GIMS
  • Kidney transplant surgery provided free of cost under its programme
  • Post-transplant medicines included in the institute's stated support

The availability of a transplant, the suitability of a donor, and a patient's eligibility for an individual programme depend on specialist assessment and current institutional requirements.

Serving patients beyond Khairpur

GIMS is based in Gambat, District Khairpur, in Upper Sindh. In its social media statement, the institute highlights its services for people from Sindh, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, as well as Afghanistan and Iran.

The announcement does not provide a breakdown of how many transplant recipients came from each region. Its significance is the institute's reported expansion of access to specialist kidney care outside Pakistan's largest metropolitan centres.

Why post-transplant care matters

A kidney transplant is not the end of treatment. Recipients typically require continuing specialist follow-up, laboratory monitoring and immunosuppressive medicines to reduce the risk of rejection. Missing prescribed medicines can place the transplanted kidney at risk. Patients should follow their transplant team's individual instructions.

Anyone considering kidney transplantation should seek assessment from an authorised transplant centre and ask about donor evaluation, treatment eligibility, follow-up care and any programme-specific conditions.
