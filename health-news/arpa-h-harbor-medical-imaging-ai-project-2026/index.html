US ARPA-H HARBOR Project: Up to $42 Million for Medical Imaging AI Research

ARPA-H lists a HARBOR award of up to $42 million for a platform helping developers work with deidentified radiology and pathology data.

What is HARBOR?

The US Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H) lists a HARBOR award starting 16 November 2026 for up to $42 million. HARBOR stands for Health Asset Registry for Bioimaging, Orchestration, & Reuse. It is intended to make deidentified radiology and pathology images more useful for developing and evaluating medical AI.

Why access to imaging data matters

Developers need representative, well-curated data to test whether imaging algorithms perform reliably across clinical settings. Hospital archives can be large but fragmented and difficult to use for research. HARBOR aims to support data discovery, preparation, evaluation and collaboration without promising a particular diagnostic breakthrough today.

How privacy is addressed

ARPA-H describes federated-learning and data-access technologies intended to keep deidentified hospital data secured within participating hospital systems. Deidentification and access controls reduce privacy risks but must be evaluated continuously; the programme is not an assurance that every privacy risk disappears.

Not an approved diagnostic device

This is funding for infrastructure and research. HARBOR is not itself a newly approved scanner, a new cancer test, or a treatment offered to patients. The announced US project does not establish any Pakistani institutional involvement.

Frequently asked questions

Is HARBOR a new medical scan available to patients?

No. It is an AI research data infrastructure project, not a clinical device or treatment.

How much funding is listed?

ARPA-H lists an award of up to US$42 million beginning 16 November 2026.

Will patient images be publicly released?

The programme describes secured deidentified data and federated methods, not unrestricted publication of identifiable hospital images.
