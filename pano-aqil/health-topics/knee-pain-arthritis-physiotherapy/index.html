Understanding knee pain

Knee pain can result from an injury, overuse or arthritis. Osteoarthritis may cause stiffness, movement discomfort and difficulty climbing stairs. A recent injury or a newly hot swollen knee needs different evaluation from chronic joint symptoms.

How physiotherapy may help

For diagnosed knee osteoarthritis, clinical guidance recommends appropriately tailored strengthening and aerobic activity. An assessment helps select safe exercises based on function and pain. Physiotherapy is not a cure for arthritis and should not delay medical treatment for acute injuries.

Warning signs needing prompt care

Urgently seek care after major injury if the knee is deformed or weight-bearing is impossible. A very hot swollen joint with fever requires urgent assessment because joint infection is possible. New severe swelling or rapidly worsening pain should be checked.

Questions about knee pain

Can exercise support knee osteoarthritis?

Yes, a regular, tailored exercise programme can improve strength and function, although it may need adjustment if symptoms flare.

Does every swollen knee require physiotherapy?

No. A new swollen or hot joint, especially with fever or injury, should be medically assessed first.
