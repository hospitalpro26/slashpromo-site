Why is rehabilitation different after an injury or operation?

The safe timing of movement depends on the injury, any surgical repair and instructions from the treating surgeon. Some people need restricted weight-bearing, bracing or a protected range of motion before strengthening begins. A published physiotherapist profile does not mean every treatment is appropriate for every injury.

What may a rehabilitation assessment include?

A physiotherapist may review the discharge or referral advice, pain, swelling, range of movement, muscle strength, balance, walking and activities important to the patient. A progressive programme may include appropriate mobility work, assisted walking, exercise and functional training when medically cleared. Progress varies; there is no one fixed recovery schedule.

When should someone return to a doctor urgently?

Seek urgent medical assessment for new or increasing limb weakness, severe worsening pain, fever, a red or draining surgical wound, or new significant swelling. Sudden breathlessness, chest pain or coughing blood requires emergency care; a swollen painful calf after surgery may need prompt evaluation for a blood clot. Do not attempt aggressive exercises before medical clearance.

Common questions

Can I start physiotherapy immediately after surgery?

Not necessarily. The operation, fixation, healing and surgeon's instructions determine which movements are safe and when they should start.

Will physiotherapy replace my surgeon's follow-up?

No. Rehabilitation and the treating doctor's follow-up have different roles and should be coordinated.
