Back Pain and Sciatica: Physiotherapy in Shikarpur

Back pain, radiating leg pain and sciatica are not always the same problem. Learn when to seek assessment and what rehabilitation may involve.

How is back pain different from sciatica?

Lower-back pain may follow muscle strain, stiffness or other conditions. Sciatica usually refers to pain that travels from the lower back or buttock down a leg and may come with tingling or altered sensation. Symptoms alone cannot establish the cause; a clinician can assess strength, sensation, movement and other warning signs.

When might physiotherapy help?

Depending on assessment, physiotherapy may involve advice on remaining active, graduated walking or exercise, movement and posture strategies, and a rehabilitation plan tailored to daily activities. Prolonged bed rest is generally discouraged for uncomplicated back pain. Exercises should be adapted if they worsen symptoms.

When is medical or emergency assessment needed?

Seek immediate emergency care for new problems controlling the bladder or bowel, numbness around the genitals or bottom, severe or increasing weakness in both legs, or back pain after major trauma. Arrange prompt medical evaluation for fever, unexplained weight loss, worsening night pain or rapidly progressing symptoms. A physiotherapy directory is not a substitute for emergency assessment.

Common questions

Does everyone with back pain need an MRI?

No. Imaging is not routinely needed for every episode of uncomplicated back pain. The clinician decides whether tests are necessary based on symptoms and examination.

Can I start exercises without assessment?

Gentle activity can be helpful, but exercises should be adjusted to your symptoms. Seek professional advice if symptoms are severe, worsening or accompanied by warning signs.
