Frozen Shoulder and Shoulder Stiffness in Shikarpur

Understand persistent shoulder stiffness, gradual recovery, safe movement and when to seek an examination.

What does a frozen shoulder feel like?

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) can cause persistent pain and a gradual loss of shoulder movement, sometimes interfering with dressing, reaching overhead or sleeping. Other shoulder problems can look similar, so a healthcare professional should assess persistent or severe pain rather than assuming the diagnosis.

How can physiotherapy support recovery?

Rehabilitation may include gentle mobility work, individually selected stretches, gradually increased strengthening and advice about everyday activity. The programme should reflect whether pain or stiffness is the main problem; forcing painful movements may make symptoms worse. Recovery can take many months, and physiotherapy does not offer an instant cure.

Who should seek clinical assessment?

Arrange an assessment if shoulder pain or stiffness is persistent, worsening or stopping daily tasks. Seek urgent medical assessment after a fall or injury with deformity, inability to move the arm, numbness, or fever and a hot swollen joint. A clinician can also consider other causes such as arthritis or tendon disorders.

Common questions

How long does frozen shoulder take to recover?

Symptoms may last months and sometimes longer. A rehabilitation professional can adjust a gradual plan, while a doctor may discuss pain control or other treatments if needed.

Is any shoulder pain a frozen shoulder?

No. Tendon, joint, nerve and injury-related problems can also cause shoulder pain; professional examination helps distinguish them.
